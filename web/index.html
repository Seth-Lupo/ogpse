For every contiguous land holding over 2,000 acres in Pecos County, Texas: how many megawatts of round-the-clock power an off-grid solar and battery campus could deliver, and the capital cost per firm megawatt.

Method

  1. Sites. TxGIO 2026 parcels merged into contiguous same-owner holdings; holdings of 2,000+ acres are candidate sites.
  2. Buildable land. Remove 10 m cells over the tracker slope limit (USGS 3DEP lidar DEM; 8.5° N–S per the Array DuraTrack datasheet), NWI wetlands, and FEMA flood zones. Unmapped flood areas are flagged, not assumed safe.
  3. Layout. Fill each buildable north–south run with 1-in-portrait tracker rows of 27-module strings at a fixed pitch; drop rows that tilt beyond the tracker limit. Sum MWdc.
  4. Solar output. pvlib single-axis tracking with backtracking, Perez sky, CEC single-diode module and Sandia inverter models, hourly over 2000–2024 NSRDB weather.
  5. Sizing. Per MW of flat load, search battery power and duration; for each, bisect the smallest PV that keeps the hourly state-of-charge simulation at 99.9% of hours served. Keep the cheapest. Uptime counts hours, which an optimizer could only express with one binary variable per hour (219,000); it rises monotonically with PV, so bisection finds the minimum directly.
  6. Rank. Firm MW = site MWdc ÷ PV per firm MW. Cost = NREL ATB 2025 equipment cost plus land. The driver column shows whether weather or land puts a site above the county's best.

Limits

Hourly energy balance only; no electrical stability, degradation, or battery replacement. Capital cost only, not lifetime cost. One tracker, module, and battery type. Land priced at one fixed $/acre. Weather shared across a 0.2° grid. Pecos County has no FEMA flood maps. No soils, fiber, oil and gas, or easement layers.
